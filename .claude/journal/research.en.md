However, as an underwater analogue of legged-robot push-recovery it is **valid as a robustness/recovery test**. Report it separately as a "recovery test", not as "a disturbance the observer compensates".
- **Verified (numbers)**:
  - Actual coastal/tidal turbulent forcing = O(0.1–10 N) (re-derived with the sim's own coefficients Xu_dot=5.5, Xuu=18.18), 1–2 orders of magnitude smaller than the kick's 20–50N. Time scale of seconds to tens of seconds (integral timescale ~6s, Milne 2017 RSPA), not 0.15s → the kick is ~10–300× shorter.
  - Tidal turbulence intensity TI ~6–10% (Thomson et al. 2012 IEEE JOE 37(3):363-374) — tens-of-% is the tail.
  - Vortex shedding: 1–5N, period 3–8s (St·U/D), not an impulse. Slamming: vanishes when fully submerged (3m).
  - DOB/ESO designs assume d_dot≈0 (Chen et al. 2000 IEEE TIE 47(4):932-938; Do&Nguyen 2018 IEEE Access). The project's EAOB also uses w_dot=0 (eaob.py) → a 0.15s impulse is the maximal violation of that assumption → d_hat cannot cancel it cleanly (eval_dp.py also records "0.15s kicks not rejected").
  - The push-recovery protocol (short force pulses at random times, mostly horizontal) is a standard robustness benchmark (arXiv 2407.04224 PA-LOCO, 2104.14534, 2203.01148).
- **Questionable (weaknesses)**: ① torque-free (applied at the COM) — real impulses (collision · tether snatch) are off-COM and so induce yaw/pitch torque; this omission is the biggest defect → the results are optimistic. ② The 20–50N magnitude · 0.15s duration have no environmental-disturbance anchor (engineering default); justified only as a contact/worst-case probe.
- **Recommendation**: keep the kick, but (1) relabel it as an "unmodeled impulsive perturbation/push-recovery test" (do not call it turbulence), (2) report it separately on a recovery axis (peak excursion+settling time), (3) highest-value upgrade = apply it off-COM to impart torque, (4) for environmental realism, do not enlarge the kick — add a colored gust velocity (von Karman/Dryden, tau~L/U~1–30s, TI~6–10%) to the water-velocity, (5) demonstrate the observer's steady-state cancellation with an optional constant-bias case.
